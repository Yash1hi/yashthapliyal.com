I think that this month has been particularly jarring to my psyche when watching the state of the world change. I would love to believe that the generations before me have meaningful advice, but I don't think they've ever dealt with such exponential growth before.

1. The midterms have cause the USA to swing blue in both The House and the Senate. There are a couple of notable DSA leaders who I can see swinging the way American politics works in the long run (Zohran Mamdani, Abdul El-Sayed, Nithya Raman).

2. Flooding in San Francisco, particularly my district of the Mission, unlike anything seen before. I'd like to believe this is because of the Super El Niño, but part of me is drawn towards the very real effects of climate change.

3. Gemini 4 Neon, Grokbot, and Opus 5.5/Fable 5.2 gradually becoming easier to use and more powerful than I've seen before. 

4. Formalized Lean Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis (Law(?)), as well as several other notable math problems.

5. AI generated long form video animations and realistic 3d modeling.

6. Anthropic IPO.

7. Jensen Huang erasing and bringing back a couple trillion in market cap through his affairs.

8. GTA 6 has been released, alongside PC decompilation being nearly complete in less than 3 days.

9. Mimikyu93 bot + Solving VGC pokemon.

10. PHP2Shell, and various other security vulnerabilities across all platforms.

I am in no way saying that these are all of the same value in my head, just that they have all wildly moved me in one way or another. They have all also happened relatively quickly, slow enough to let me understand them but fast enough to not let me process their implications. I'm not sure where the world will go from here to be honest. At times, it feels like I'm sitting at the forefront of what is the norm, and most of the world has not caught up yet. I don't believe in the old fucks in charge of the US to do anything useful in this field, where disinformation is rampant and unbiased advisorship seems scarce.

Part of the complications of this is that I'm relatively new to the field, and to life itself. I haven't gone through the waves that others have, and I haven't witnessed firsthand enough to say that this is dissimilar to previous events. Maybe I'm just 21. At the same time, I feel that if most of the world had the knowledge I have, we would operate radically differently on a day-to-day, which seems to be non-resonant with my ideas that most people know more than me.

Here are some bets on what I think will happen in the next few months to year:

1. The formalization of AI-agents in most workflows. Most people just do not have the understanding of capability that these models have. The automation of nearly all manually inputted digital work is ridiculous. It should happen, and the bottleneck feels like users (and privacy but...)

2. Privacy will never bounce back. In the process of our sprint towards the horizon of intelligence, we gave up much of the meaning of personal privacy. With near infinite skill that's parallelized, the vast data pools that are associated with people can be queried to find anything about them. I don't think laws will protect the consumer much anymore.

3. The sentiment towards AI will become less negative as a whole. While the economic investment is huge, this technology has and will continue to change the world. On a day-to-day for people who operate at all in a digital space, it changes the way you can interact and understand anything. These models are good enough locally (e.g. inkling 2) that there is little reason to go back.

4. DSA will become a prominent party, and the establishment democratic will slowly split. I don't know enough about politics to say this as a fact, it's just a gut feeling fueled by a (what may be bot infused) internet. I also think SF may flip red.

5. Software will become more closed off. We will lose access to source code, to running our own software, etc. I don't think this will make SWE less valuable, but something like zero-access policy across the internet feels feasible. Devices may become thinner on the client side and move to more server-side processing.

6. Increased polarization in the world. I've already seen it increase handedly in my lifetime, but I think we are at the tip of the iceberg.

Today, I am wildly more productive than I've ever been in my life. My skills and capabilities are far beyond what I believed I could do at 21. At the same time, I am the least confident in my future, or the futures of anyone I know around me. It feels like there has been wild disruption in every major field in the world, and the universal state of global power is unclear to me so I cannot point people to a correct direction. I consider myself an optimistic person, and I'm excited to see what the future is, even if I'm not sure what that direction could be.
